# Chapter 1: Introduction to Lambda-CDM

The Lambda-CDM ($\Lambda\text{CDM}$) model is the standard paradigm of modern big bang cosmology. It is frequently referred to as the **Standard Model of Cosmology** because it provides a remarkably successful phenomenological fit to a wide array of cosmic observations, including:

* The Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) anisotropies.
* The large-scale distribution of galaxies (galaxy clustering).
* The luminosity distances of high-redshift Type Ia supernovae.

## Historical Context

The model builds upon Albert Einstein's introduction of the cosmological constant ($\Lambda$) in 1917, which he originally formulated to achieve a static universe. Following Edwin Hubble's observation of galactic recession, the static universe was abandoned, and $\Lambda$ was largely set to zero. 

However, the discovery of accelerating cosmic expansion in 1998 via distant supernovae forced the resurrection of $\Lambda$—reinterpreted not as a geometric fudge factor, but as **dark energy** possessing negative pressure.
