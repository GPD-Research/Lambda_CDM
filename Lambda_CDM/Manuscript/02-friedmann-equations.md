# Chapter 2: The Friedmann Equations

The background spacetime of $\Lambda\text{CDM}$ is described by the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric, assuming a spatially flat universe ($k = 0$). 

## The Expansion Rate

The evolution of the Hubble parameter $H(z)$ as a function of redshift $z$ is governed by the first Friedmann equation:

$$H^2(z) = H_0^2 \left[ \Omega_{r,0}(1+z)^4 + \Omega_{m,0}(1+z)^3 + \Omega_{\Lambda,0} \right]$$

Where the key cosmological parameters at present day ($z = 0$) are:

* $H_0$: The Hubble constant (expansion rate today).
* $\Omega_{r,0}$: Radiation density parameter (photons and relativistic neutrinos).
* $\Omega_{m,0}$: Matter density parameter (cold dark matter + baryonic matter).
* $\Omega_{\Lambda,0}$: Dark energy density parameter (cosmological constant).

## Energy Conservation Components

Each component scales differently with the scale factor $a(t) = \frac{1}{1+z}$:

1. **Radiation:** $\rho_r \propto a^{-4}$ (due to energy dilution and adiabatic redshift of photons).
2. **Matter:** $\rho_m \propto a^{-3}$ (volume dilution).
3. **Dark Energy:** $\rho_\Lambda \propto a^0$ (constant energy density).
