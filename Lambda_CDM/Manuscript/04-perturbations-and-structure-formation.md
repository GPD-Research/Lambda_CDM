# Chapter 4: Perturbations and Structure Formation

While the background universe is homogeneous and isotropic, cosmic structure (galaxies, clusters, filaments) arises from tiny primordial density perturbations, quantified by density contrast:

$$\delta(\mathbf{x}, t) = \frac{\rho(\mathbf{x}, t) - \bar{\rho}(t)}{\bar{\rho}(t)}$$

## Cold Dark Matter (CDM) Role

Baryonic matter alone could not have collapsed fast enough to form observed galaxies by the present era due to radiation pressure prior to recombination. **Cold Dark Matter** acts as a gravitational scaffold:

* CDM is "cold," meaning it moves at non-relativistic speeds ($\text{v} \ll c$) early on.
* It decouples from radiation early, allowing gravitational potential wells to grow well before atoms combine.
