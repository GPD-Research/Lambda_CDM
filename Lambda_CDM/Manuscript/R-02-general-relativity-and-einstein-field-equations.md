# Chapter R-2: General Relativity and the Einstein Field Equations

Special relativity applies only in the absence of gravity (flat spacetime). To describe a dynamic, expanding universe filled with matter and energy, we must transition to **General Relativity (1915)**, which geometrized gravity as the curvature of spacetime.

## Spacetime Curvature and the Metric

In general relativity, space and time are no longer a static stage. They form a curved four-dimensional manifold described by a general metric tensor $g_{\mu\nu}$. The invariant interval generalizes to:

$$ds^2 = g_{\mu\nu} dx^\mu dx^\nu$$

Matter and energy tell spacetime how to curve, and curved spacetime tells matter and energy how to move (via the geodesic equation).

## The Einstein Field Equations

The mathematical bridge connecting the geometry of spacetime to its energy content is expressed in the **Einstein Field Equations**:

$$G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}$$

Where each term represents a critical pillar of modern cosmology:

1. **The Einstein Tensor ($G_{\mu\nu}$):** Encapsulates the local curvature of spacetime. It is constructed from the Ricci tensor ($R_{\mu\nu}$), the Ricci scalar ($R$), and the metric tensor:
   $$G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} R$$
2. **The Cosmological Constant ($\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$):** Originally introduced by Einstein to keep the universe static, now interpreted as the intrinsic vacuum energy (dark energy) driving cosmic acceleration.
3. **Newton's Gravitational Constant ($G$) and Speed of Light ($c$):** Fundamental coupling constants scaling the geometric side to the physical matter side.
4. **The Stress-Energy Tensor ($T_{\mu\nu}$):** Represents the distribution and density of matter, radiation, pressure, and momentum throughout the cosmos.
